The Lead

Oklahoma's troll playlist got the clicks. The real story from Norman is what it ended and what it built: Ole Miss' playoff run is over by Pete Golding's own admission, and Jason Witten's run-it-49-times offense now looks like a real identity.

Game week is here. The hinge is LSU at Tennessee (3:30 p.m. ET), where Lane Kiffin returns to Neyland as a 1.5-point favorite in a game the market calls a coin flip.

Top stories

Oklahoma

Witten ran it 49 times, and now he's in the OC conversation.

OU threw 19 passes in 68 plays, ran for 188 yards and held the ball nearly 37 minutes for the second straight week. Witten took over play-calling Nov. 2 after Ben Arbuckle was fired; before this season, he'd coached only high school. CBS Sports, OU game notes

Why it matters: OU (6-4, 4-3) is bowl eligible, and the identity travels: Texas A&M comes to Norman with an offense Mike Elko called "nonfunctional." A ball-control team with an elite defense is the worst possible draw for a team that can't score.

Ole Miss

Golding says the playoff is gone and puts his own job on the table.

"This seals the fate from a playoff standpoint." On whether he'll give up defensive play-calling: "Everything's definitely on the table." On roster motives: "If it's just about money, why you play this game, I don't want you." HottyToddy.com

Why it matters: Ole Miss is 6-4 (4-4), and Golding called third down "really the Achilles' heel." A first-year head coach openly weighing a coordinator split in mid-November is a sign the offseason has already started in Oxford.

Oklahoma

Bob Stoops skipped the suite for the slop.

OU's "Program guy" photo of Stoops at field level in the rain drew more than 8,000 likes. On the radio, per Chris Oven: "I couldn't challenge the OU fanbase to get out there in the cold and rain, and then be up in the suite." Parker Thune also reported OU played Tom Petty's "Learning to Fly," a second troll song on top of "Wheels on the Bus." Oklahoma on X, Chris Oven on X, Parker Thune on X

Why it matters: The fan anger was real, and Venables credited it.

South Carolina

Beamer fires back over Sellers, and the SEC backs him.

LaNorris Sellers was listed "probable," warmed up with the first team and never played in the 34-6 loss. Beamer: "It means that he's probable to be available." The SEC "found no basis to conclude that South Carolina violated" its availability policy. On3

Why it matters: Georgia comes to Columbia Saturday night, and Cutter Woods threw 3 picks in relief. "Available but not playing" will be the most-parsed phrase on this week's report.

Arkansas

The field storm cost $500,000.

The SEC fined Arkansas for fans rushing the field after the 34-6 win over South Carolina, despite a video-board request to wait three minutes. The money goes to South Carolina. It's at least the fourth SEC field-storming fine this season, after Kentucky, Tennessee and South Carolina. SEC, RG.org

Why it matters: South Carolina lost by 28 and still gets a half-million-dollar check. The fine is a ticket price at this point, not a deterrent.

Tennessee

Matthews is done, and MacIntyre is the new watch item.

Mike Matthews (39 catches, 608 yards, 4 TDs) is expected to miss the rest of the season with a knee injury, per On3's Pete Nakos and Chris Low. Heupel pulled George MacIntyre in the second half at A&M because of an injury: "Didn't feel like it was the right place for George." On3

Why it matters: Tennessee (8-2, 5-2) needs LSU and Vanderbilt to stay in the playoff picture, and it just lost its top receiver.

LSU at Tennessee

Kiffin's homecoming opens as a near pick'em.

LSU is -1.5 (total 45.5) on the BetIQ market board, with moneylines nearly even (LSU -111, Tennessee -108). It's Kiffin's second trip back to Knoxville as a head coach. Heupel at SEC Media Days in July: "Hopefully Knoxville's ready for his arrival." BetIQ, Rocky Top Insider

Why it matters: LSU's playoff hopes are dead, and it can take Tennessee's down with it.

Texas A&M

The kickoff moved for Oklahoma, not for A&M.

The SEC pushed A&M at OU from noon to 3:45 p.m. ET. Venables: "I'll take every minute we can get." ESPN

Why it matters: A&M (4-6) has to win out to make a bowl. Josh Pate made the Aggies "This week's Sarah McLachlan Special." Josh Pate on X

Deep dive: The Sunday that ended one season and saved another

The facts

Oklahoma beat No. 19 Ole Miss 24-17 in the rain on Sunday, a day late after Friday's FAA ground stop. OU ran 49 times for 188 yards and threw only 19 passes in 68 plays. It averaged just 4.46 yards per play but held the ball for nearly 37 minutes, as it did against Florida a week earlier, per CBS Sports. Witten has called plays since Nov. 2, per OU game notes. Golding blamed missed tackles, third downs ("third-and-six-plus, a lot of them") and a punt-return TD, and said of OU's run game: "It was never the run." HottyToddy.com Golding also said Ole Miss sat on buses for three hours on Friday, and Venables said the crowd was "loud, engaged and pissed." ESPN Before Sunday, ESPN's FPI had OU No. 23 and Ole Miss No. 13, per Bama Hammer.

Analysis

Ole Miss didn't lose this game on the bus. It lost it on third-and-7.

Golding's own numbers make the case: OU wasn't gashing the Rebels, it was simply never getting off the field. That's what Witten built. Take the decisions away from John Mateer, run it with three tight ends, and make the opponent's best players stand on the sideline.

That style doesn't blow anybody out. It doesn't have to. Against A&M, which managed 179 yards against Tennessee, a 39.5 total and a 7.5-point spread both make sense. If Oklahoma gets an early lead, that game could be over by the third quarter.

For Ole Miss, the bigger story is the press conference. "Everything's on the table," plus "I don't want you" for players who are in it for the money, plus a portal window and roster retention meetings coming up. That's a coach drawing lines in November. Golding giving up defensive play-calling is the move. A head coach who's also the coordinator can't fix missed tackles and run a program at the same time.

My take: If Ole Miss was hoping the delay would help, it didn't. The game was played in the rain, which suits how Oklahoma wants to play with Witten calling plays, and the delay made the crowd angry. I'll say this, though: I loved a standalone SEC game on a Sunday. With 138 FBS teams, Saturdays feel like drinking from a firehose. And the SEC threw OU a bone by moving next week's kickoff back.

  • Oklahoma (Brent Venables / Jason Witten): Two straight wins with Witten calling plays. CBS says Venables has "a tough decision this offseason" on whether to hire an OC. CBS Sports

Nobody will let LSU cry "poor me" after that roster spend, though, and I don't know how they rally in Knoxville against a team that wants to punch them in the face 50 times.

Kentucky hoops

All eyes on Rupp tonight (Grambling State, 7 p.m. ET, SECN+)

Friday, Kentucky beat Northern Arizona 78-77. NAU came in 0-3 and closed as roughly a 30.5-point underdog. Yahoo, Action Network

NAU led 77-73 with 22 seconds left. Alex Wilkins bailed out UK with a 3 at 2.2 seconds, and "Fire Mark Pope" chants had already started. USA Today

Pope: "I hope everybody in Rupp had fun." Rupp mostly heard it as a dare. UK Athletics

The Kansas loss was 89-69. KenPom has UK at No. 54, down from No. 26 in the preseason, and 12th of 16 SEC teams. That isn't an at-large profile, even with a bigger field. A Sea of Blue (preseason), KenPom

Kentucky has been here before. Calipari's last years had a 9-16 season with no tournament in 2021, then first-round losses to No. 15 Saint Peter's in 2022 and No. 14 Oakland in 2024. 247Sports, Yahoo

Pope is 46-26 (20-16 SEC). If he's fired before April 1, 2027, he's owed 75% of the $17.25 million left on his deal, a shade under $13 million. USA Today

My take: No way Pope survives if they miss the tournament.

Next real test: Indiana, Friday, 8 p.m. ET, CBS, Lucas Oil Stadium.
