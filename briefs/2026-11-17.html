The Lead

LSU had the kind of Monday that makes you check whether the SEC office has a "do not disturb" sign. Lane Kiffin swore twice describing his nightmares, his quarterback's future is suddenly a debate and the league told RJ Luis Jr.'s lawyers in writing that it won't punish LSU for rostering him.

Tonight the committee releases its third CFP rankings (9 p.m. ET, ESPN). Saturday's biggest SEC game is still LSU at Tennessee, where Kiffin plays spoiler in the stadium where fans once threw mustard at him.

Rankings are AP (Nov. 16) unless noted. The last CFP rankings are from Nov. 10.

Top stories

LSU

Kiffin's presser turned into a confession.

He said he woke up every hour for two nights reliving Arch Manning's 4-yard game-winner to a wide-open Raleek Brown: "And then I wake up, and it's real." And the line that went viral: "We're not gonna f***ing cover the guy and he's gonna walk in the f***ing end zone?" He even said he keeps seeing himself call the timeout he never called. The Athletic (Matt Baker), Mike Scarborough on X The Advocate's Scott Rabalais: in 35 years covering LSU, "I've never heard a coach drop two F-bombs in a press conference." Rabalais on X

Why it matters: LSU (6-4, 3-4) is out of the CFP race and plays at No. 9 Tennessee as a 1.5-point favorite. Kiffin's other line, "I'm not worried about a thing," is the one that will age either very well or very badly. The Advocate

LSU

Sam Leavitt's future is suddenly a debate.

He leads the SEC in passing yards (2,797) and interceptions (12). Against Texas he threw three picks, two in the end zone. Kiffin: "really poor decisions... He's worried about throwing a fourth interception. So he can kind of spiral on you." Kiffin won't give the backups extra snaps: "My job is to go 1-0 that week." The Advocate, Scarborough on X USA Today reported students leaving Tiger Stadium chanting "(Expletive) Sam Leavitt!" and said Leavitt was seen leaving the field before the alma mater. USA Today

The other side: LSU Odyssey's Lonn Phillips, citing unnamed "top level sources," wrote that a Leavitt return is "just about impossible," though no LSU beat writer has reported that. Lonn Phillips on X CBS' Brad Crawford still projects Leavitt as LSU's 2027 starter, calling it the "toughest call." CBS Sports

My take: Kiffin tends to avoid taking responsibility when things go wrong. He makes excuses. He blames others. I guess it's only natural that when Sam Leavitt throws three INTs, including two in the end zone, he's going to catch some blame.

I have no idea what Leavitt is thinking (or what LSU is thinking, for that matter). He leads the SEC in passing yards... and in interceptions. When the picture defenses give him gets cloudy, he struggles badly. When he's got a clean picture, he dominates.

I doubt he gets more money from another school, and there's something to be said for continuity and stability on both sides. But can LSU bank on a QB who, at least in part, led the team to four losses (and counting) when the program is all in on being a national title contender every year?

And if either side initiates a breakup, who in the portal is better than Leavitt? Lots of intrigue, but more questions than answers right now.

Texas

Arch Manning will do senior day, and nothing else is decided.

"I'm a senior, so I'm doing it." He has one season of eligibility left and said he's "not really thinking about the future right now." He's 20-5 as a starter, and Texas (8-2, 5-2) has won four straight. ESPN Crawford projects KJ Lacey as Texas' 2027 starter, with Arch expected to turn pro after the CFP. CBS Sports Paul Finebaum on First Take: "the gutsiest quarterback I have seen since Tim Tebow." Awful Announcing

Why it matters: It's a strong signal, not a decision. Arch also threw three picks at LSU and still won.

My take: The Brad Crawford story suggests maybe Arch goes pro. Earlier this season, the prevailing media and fan sentiment was that Texas and Manning needed to part ways one way or another.

But what happens if Texas finishes 10-2, maybe plays in the final SEC title game and maybe wins a game or two in the Playoff... and the Manning family, known to value the college experience (see: Manning, Peyton), wants Arch to wait for the 2028 NFL draft? Can you really hit the eject button on Arch for his backup then?

SEC

Crawford's 2027 QB board has four teams going shopping.

CBS projects a transfer starter at Arkansas, Auburn, Oklahoma and Texas A&M. The portal opens Jan. 4. Two lines stand out: "The ship has sailed on Marcel Reed as Texas A&M's starter in 2027," and Mississippi State's Kamario Taylor is back on a deal "in excess of $6 million." On A&M, Crawford says who calls plays "might be more important than who's playing quarterback." CBS Sports

The most significant projection may be South Carolina: Crawford has LaNorris Sellers returning, writing that "a first-round NFL Draft grade might be the only thing that gets [him] out of Columbia." Multiple sources told CBS that South Carolina and Sellers' camp have opened conversations about a 2027 return. Crawford says there's reason for both sides to keep talking "as long as OC Kendal Briles stays put," and that the chance to play alongside his brother Jayden, who is coming back from a season-ending knee injury, "carries weight." If Sellers does get a first-round grade, "he's not expected to come back."

The rest of Crawford's board: Alabama, Keelon Russell (returning starter); Florida, Aaron Philo (returning starter, though an insider called the plan "50-50"); Georgia, Ryan Puglisi (backup takes over as Gunner Stockton departs); Kentucky, Kenny Minchey (returning starter); Missouri, Austin Simmons (returning starter); Ole Miss, Deuce Knight (backup; Trinidad Chambliss is finishing his career, and Crawford says it could still go to the portal); Tennessee, George MacIntyre (the current starter over five-star freshman Faizon Brandon, and "one of the hardest projections"); Vanderbilt, Jared Curtis (returning starter).

Why it matters: Crawford warns this could be one of the weaker QB markets in years. If Sellers stays, South Carolina keeps its most important piece. If he goes, the Gamecocks join the shopping list. (Opinion: four SEC buyers in a thin market means someone overpays for a one-year rental, and A&M has the money to be that someone.)

Texas A&M

Elko admits it's unraveling.

"You've seen it unravel to some degree. Lack of confidence. Lack of continuity. Lack of flow." On benching Reed vs. Tennessee: "That was a decision I made." On the portal: "an on-going evaluation process for us on how to do it the right way." Aggies Wire SI added him to its hot seat index. A&M went 11-2 last year and is 4-6 (1-6) now. SI

Why it matters: To make a bowl, A&M has to win at Oklahoma, where it's a 7.5-point underdog, and then beat Texas.

Deep dive: The SEC blinked on RJ Luis. It didn't fold.

The facts

The SEC told Luis' lawyers it "will abide by the terms of the preliminary injunction while it remains in place and will not impose penalties or fines on LSU if it places Mr. Luis on its men's basketball roster." CBS Sports (Matt Norlander), Matt Moscona on X An SEC source told CBS the league has no intention of giving up the fight. Luis' attorney: "RJ is healthy, ready and looking forward to joining his teammates." As of the CBS story, LSU and Wade hadn't commented. In October, Wade said, "We're going to follow all the SEC protocols with him." Luis hasn't played college basketball since March 2025. He'd join Donovan Dent, Skyy Clark and Austin Nunez as LSU players who needed favorable court rulings to play this season. CBS Sports Meanwhile, the SEC's federal lawsuit against LSU is still pending in Alabama, and the league's own filing cites LSU's handling of Luis as evidence of "ongoing defiance." The suit also asks the court to keep LSU from blocking a vote on ending its membership if the league ever holds one. Ending a membership takes a two-thirds vote of presidents, and none is scheduled. SECfb.com Ross Dellenger noted that the league said something similar about football players Dae'Quan Wright and Zxavian Harris, and that LSU didn't roster them. Dellenger on X

Analysis

The SEC didn't lose an argument on Monday. It ran out of ways to avoid one. Its statement is narrow: no fines or penalties while the injunction stands. That clause carries the whole statement. The lawsuit is still alive, the expulsion clause still exists and Luis is now Exhibit A in a case the league says isn't moot.

So the risk moves from the basketball court to the president's office. Wade's math is easy: LSU (4-0) is No. 25 in the AP poll and No. 23 in KenPom, and CBS says Luis could give Wade one of the 15 to 20 best rosters in the country. LSU's leadership has a harder question: is one wing worth handing the SEC a cleaner fact pattern in federal court?

My take: I'm not a lawyer. I also don't know the bigger picture. Will this put LSU in more jeopardy with the SEC, which threatened to kick it out of the conference earlier this fall, or invite more legal action down the road? Do the higher-ups and stakeholders above Wade have objections because of political or SEC pressure?

If Wade gets the all-clear from legal and from his university leadership, then absolutely, he should play him. Coaches are paid to win by any (legal) means necessary. Wade was making "strong-a**" offers before it was legal. Yahoo Sports (2019) So yes, he should play Luis if he gets a green light. This LSU team is already a complete mercenary unit anyway.

🦹 Quick Top 5: The greatest college football villains of all time (my list)

  1. Steve Spurrier. A Heisman winner at Florida who coached like a stand-up act. He called Florida State "Free Shoes University" and said Peyton Manning came back for his senior year to be "a three-time Citrus Bowl MVP." Wikipedia
  2. Miami, late 1980s and early '90s. In the 1991 Cotton Bowl, Miami set records with 15 penalties for 202 yards, many for unsportsmanlike conduct. The NCAA's excessive-celebration penalty grew partly out of that game. Miami then went 12-0 and won the next AP title. Wikipedia
  3. Lane Kiffin. He lasted one season at Tennessee (2009) before leaving for USC, and he left Ole Miss for LSU in 2025 while his team was preparing for the Playoff. Wikipedia Fans threw mustard at him in Knoxville in 2021. The Athletic Saturday, he's back.
  4. Brian Bosworth. Barred from the 1987 Orange Bowl after a positive steroid test, he wore a "NCAA: National Communists Against Athletes" T-shirt on the sideline, and Oklahoma dismissed him. Wikipedia
  5. Nick Saban's Alabama. Six national titles in Tuscaloosa (2009, 2011, 2012, 2015, 2017, 2020). The villainy was the volume. Wikipedia
  • Honorable mentions: Johnny Manziel (the first freshman Heisman winner, then suspended for a half after the autograph investigation) Wikipedia; Michigan with Jim Harbaugh, Connor Stalions and Sherrone Moore (the 2023 sign-stealing scandal) Wikipedia; Cam Newton (one Auburn season: a Heisman, a national title and a one-day ineligibility ruling over his father's solicitation) Wikipedia.
  • Current villains: Curt Cignetti, who turned the losingest program in college football history into national champions Wikipedia; Deion Sanders.
