The Lead

LSU added RJ Luis Jr. to its basketball roster. The SEC called that "inconsistent with the expectations and responsibilities of membership." The two sides are due in a Birmingham courtroom this morning.

The Tigers host Louisiana-Monroe tonight. Nobody outside the building knows whether Luis Jr. will play.

Meanwhile, Texas A&M is staring at its third athletic director search since 2019 as Mike Elko finds himself on the hot seat less than one year after making the College Football Playoff.

THE BIG ONE

LSU picked a side. Now it finds out what that costs.

LSU took less than an hour from a presidential statement to a roster update. The SEC answered two hours later.

The conference can't punish LSU while a Louisiana court injunction stands. It said so in writing. But it made it crystal clear Tuesday night that it isn't happy.

Today the fight moves to federal court.

What happened

4:02 p.m. ET: LSU System President Wade Rousse posted a statement saying the school is "committed to compliance with NCAA and SEC rules, but it is equally committed to obeying the orders of Louisiana's courts."

LSU's general counsel told the SEC the school is in an "untenable situation" with "no viable alternative" but to comply with the Nov. 13 injunction.

4:45 p.m. ET: LSU added Luis Jr. to its official roster: No. 12, 6-7, fifth-year senior.

6:58 p.m. ET: The SEC's official reply: "This is a deeply concerning and disappointing development."

What the reporters are saying

ESPN's Pete Thamel put it plainest: "Don't expect this to be the last we hear of this matter. The SEC has made it very clear that they don't want programs rostering former pro players."

Seth Davis went further. The court order "does not require LSU to roster Luis," he wrote. "All it says is that the SEC is not allowed to punish LSU if it chooses to." Davis also said Rousse voted for the rule himself and called the president's statement "not rooted in the truth."

What's on the table

The penalties the SEC adopted in August are frozen for now. Per The Advocate's Reed Darcey, they include a half-season suspension for the coach, a fine equal to half the program's operating budget and the loss of the school's vote.

The hearing this morning involves the SEC's separate federal lawsuit against LSU. ESPN says it isn't clear what role, if any, Luis Jr. will play against ULM.

The basketball part

Lost in the legal fight: LSU is good.

Will Wade's official roster jumped from four players to 10 by Oct. 13. The media picked the Tigers 14th in the 16-team SEC. KenPom tabbed LSU at No. 47 preseason.

The Tigers are No. 23 in KenPom now at 4-0 -- before the former Big East Player of the Year who averaged 18.2 points per game for a full season plays a single minute.

Nobody's laughing at Wade's roster anymore.

My take

From a pure basketball standpoint, you'd have to consider LSU a Sweet 16 contender at this point.

Assuming Will Wade can corral all the mercenaries on the team into a good-enough culture that doesn't crack when adversity hits.

HELP WANTED

Texas A&M needs an AD. Again.

Trev Alberts signed a memorandum of understanding Tuesday night to become Michigan's athletic director per ESPN's Pete Thamel. Michigan is expected to cover his $4 million A&M buyout and he starts Dec. 1 per Yahoo's Ross Dellenger. A&M hadn't named an interim as of late Tuesday.

He's the third consecutive A&M AD to leave for another job per The Battalion: Scott Woodward to LSU (2019), Ross Bjork to Ohio State (2024) and now Alberts.

The ripple: Bruce Feldman says Michigan having an AD hired before Thanksgiving "is also probably bad news for Kyle Whittingham."

My take

Athletic directors typically have a quick hook for an underperforming coach that the previous AD hired. They love to bring in their own guys.

But it could work in the other direction as well. Because of the truncated calendar (the transfer portal opens Jan. 4), you need a new coach in place and ready to rock at some point in December. And to have a new coach in place, you need a new AD hired to run a search.

It's hard for me to imagine A&M moving on from Elko one year after he led the Aggies to the College Football Playoff and lost both coordinators and a record number of NFL draft picks. But no one expected a falloff this steep.

And calling one of the top message-board communities in the country "a cesspool" didn't help matters.

Complicating matters, Texas A&M is still paying Jimbo Fisher. His $77.6 million buyout pays about $7.27 million a year through 2031 after an initial $19.4 million payment. The Aggies still owe roughly $36 million.

Firing Elko would cost about $56.4 million as of Dec. 1, per USA Today. There's a lot of oil money in College Station. But the Aggies are better off spending any extra cash on the transfer portal to improve the roster.

Elko needs a new offensive coordinator, a new quarterback, a reworked line of scrimmage on both sides of the football and a chance to work with a new AD. If the Aggies stink again next year, the new AD can make a power move then.

WATCH THIS SPACE

LSU's other coordinator question

Wolken reports LSU defensive coordinator Blake Baker "has struggled to get on the same page with the head coach all season" per sources. He calls the relationship "rocky."

Then this: Dave Aranda "has been hanging around the building a bit" since Baylor fired him. Aranda ran LSU's defense from 2016-19.

My take

Kiffin better hope not. Baker is well-respected in media and coaching circles. LSU's defense is 10th in SP+ and seventh in EPA per snap.

LSU has enough to fix on offense, starting with whether Sam Leavitt comes back.

A lack of continuity on defense and the need to go find another defensive coordinator willing to pair with Kiffin in Baton Rouge that's at Baker's level is an extra level of drama this program doesn't need right now.
