The Lead

Georgia can end the SEC race tonight.

A win at No. 21 South Carolina makes the No. 1 Bulldogs 8-1 in league play and sends them to Atlanta for the 35th and final SEC Championship Game.

That would leave No. 3 Alabama (6-2 SEC), No. 5 Texas (5-2) and No. 9 Tennessee (5-2) fighting over the other spot.

Texas and Tennessee both play at 3:30 p.m. ET. Alabama hosts Chattanooga before next week's Iron Bowl.

The afternoon belongs to Knoxville. Lane Kiffin brings LSU back to Neyland, and the books make the unranked Tigers a slight road favorite against a Tennessee team missing a long list of starters.

Texas A&M is expected to turn to sophomore Brady Hart at No. 22 Oklahoma. The Aggies (4-6) have to beat the Sooners and Texas to reach a bowl.

Vanderbilt, 10-2 in the regular season a year ago, has to win at No. 24 Florida just to keep a 6-6 path alive.

And No. 16 Kentucky and Missouri, both on two-game losing streaks, close the night in Columbia.

Nationally, No. 13 Oregon took care of Michigan State 45-7 on Friday night.

The best non-SEC game today is No. 4 UCLA (10-0) at Michigan at 3:30 p.m. ET on CBS. No. 2 Utah (10-0), the other unbeaten in the CFP field, plays at TCU at 9 p.m. ET on Fox.

Vanderbilt (4-6, 1-6 SEC) at No. 24 Florida (6-4, 4-4 SEC)

Line: Florida -13.5 · Total: 45.5
Kickoff: noon ET · TV: SEC Network

What's at stake for Vanderbilt: The 'Dores need to win to avoid going from 10-2 in the regular season last year behind Diego Pavia to missing a bowl game altogether and perhaps finishing 4-8. That would be a true "stock down" moment for Clark Lea and would spark "same old Vandy" narratives.

What's at stake for Florida: Win Saturday and Jon Sumrall finishes his first SEC season with a winning record — the first for the Gators since 2020.

Key injuries: None

Watchability: 🥓🥓 (2 of 5 bacon strips)

Arkansas (4-6, 2-5 SEC) at No. 5 Texas (8-2, 5-2 SEC)

Line: Texas -20.5 · Total: 47.5
Kickoff: 3:30 p.m. ET · TV: ESPN

What's at stake for Arkansas: No way Arkansas can pull off two huge upsets in a row, right? Right?! Either way, even keeping this one competitive would buy Ryan Silverfield a lot more goodwill entering rivalry week after some fans were grumbling about an immediate head coaching change early in the season.

What's at stake for Texas: The Longhorns have lost as much talent to injury as any team not named Tennessee. Yet thanks to a trio of close wins, Texas is on the cusp of securing a College Football Playoff bid and is still in the mix for the SEC title game in Atlanta. But needs a win here by any means necessary.

Key injuries: Texas DE Colin Simmons remains questionable.

Watchability: 🥓🥓 (2 of 5 bacon strips)

LSU (6-4, 3-4 SEC) at No. 9 Tennessee (8-2, 5-2 SEC)

Line: LSU -1.5 · Total: 45.5
Kickoff: 3:30 p.m. ET · TV: ABC

What's at stake for LSU: Lane Kiffin can go 2-0 in Knoxville revenge tours, get LSU back into the CFP Top 25 and avoid what would become a three-game losing streak.

What's at stake for Tennessee: Tennessee can get within a win against Vanderbilt of a spot in the College Football Playoff and stay in the chase for the final SEC Championship Game.

Key injuries: - LSU LB Whit Weeks is out. CB Aiden Anding, back from a torn Achilles, is trending toward being available. - LSU WR Winston Watkins Jr. is out, per Friday night's availability report. WR Jackson Harris is questionable. - Tennessee QB George MacIntyre will be available. His start is a game-time decision, and Tennessee is readying Faizon Brandon. - LSU WR Jayce Brown didn't travel.

My take

Injuries have ravaged both teams. This is a throwback SEC football game.

The team that controls the line of scrimmage, limits turnovers (looking at you, Sam Leavitt! LSU ranks 111th nationally in giveaways per game) and physically strains more than its opponent is likely to win.

Tennessee could carry the football 50 times in this one, so LSU's impressive defense better buckle its chinstraps.

Tennessee has a lot more to play for here, while there's at least a chance of a total meltdown inside LSU's locker room.

Watchability: 🥓🥓🥓🥓🥓 (5 of 5 bacon strips)

Texas A&M (4-6, 1-6 SEC) at No. 22 Oklahoma (6-4, 4-3 SEC)

Line: Oklahoma -7 · Total: 37.5
Kickoff: 3:45 p.m. ET · TV: ESPN2

What's at stake for Texas A&M: Mike Elko is getting a head start on next season. Everyone assumes the Aggies will move on from Marcel Reed (and possibly their first-year offensive coordinator). Can Brady Hart take the defibrillator paddles and send an electrical pulse that restarts the heart of this offense? And if so, is he part of the answer for 2027?

What's at stake for Oklahoma: Jason Witten led a more conservative, ball-control offense to two fun Sooners wins after taking over play-calling duties. Football isn't played in spreadsheets. But by Dave Bartoo's opponent-adjusted numbers, the offense has performed worse than it did with Ben Arbuckle in charge. Win another one here, though, while committing to the run and ability to prevent QB John Mateer from taking fatal risks, and Witten gets that much closer to claiming the job for himself for 2027.

Key injuries: - Texas A&M is expected to start sophomore Brady Hart over Marcel Reed. Reed was 9-of-22 for 71 yards before A&M pulled him against Tennessee. - Texas A&M WR TK Norman is out.

My take

One thing's for sure: the offense was dead as a doornail with Reed.

It's not entirely his fault. The Aggies lost one of the nation's best offensive lines to the NFL, offensive coordinator Collin Klein left to become Kansas State's head coach and first-round receiver KC Concepcion moved on, too.

And I had my doubts about Reed preseason, especially after the way last season ended. But he threw for nearly 3,200 yards and rushed for another 493 and 6 TDs last season.

This season he completed just 55.7% of his passes for 8 TDs, 9 INTs and just 6.0 yards per attempt while rushing for a career-low 2.6 yards per carry.

That should close the book on Reed at Texas A&M.

Watchability: 🥓🥓 (2 of 5 bacon strips)

No. 1 Georgia (9-1, 7-1 SEC) at No. 21 South Carolina (6-4, 4-4 SEC)

Line: Georgia -14.5 · Total: 50.5
Kickoff: 7:30 p.m. ET · TV: ABC

What's at stake for Georgia: Can the Bulldogs sustain the peak that propelled them to No. 1 in the College Football Playoff rankings and the betting market futures odds? It's a long way between now and the final Playoff game. But if anyone can keep his team focused in this spot, it's Kirby Smart. A win here seals a regular-season SEC title and a spot in Atlanta.

What's at stake for South Carolina: How healthy is LaNorris Sellers? And is a great game from Sellers really best for the Gamecocks program? CBS Sports' Brad Crawford projects Sellers to return next season — unless he gets a first-round NFL Draft grade. All eyes will be on his performance against the SP+ No. 1 defense.

Key injuries: - South Carolina QB LaNorris Sellers (ankle), who missed the Arkansas game, is expected to play. - South Carolina ruled out DB Judge Collier, WR Mazeo Bennett Jr. and EDGE Anthony Addison. WR Nyck Harbor is available, and DE Dylan Stewart is questionable, per GamecockCentral.

Watchability: 🥓🥓🥓 (3 of 5 bacon strips)

No. 16 Kentucky (7-3, 5-3 SEC) at Missouri (6-4, 3-4 SEC)

Line: Missouri -4.5 · Total: 48.5
Kickoff: 7:30 p.m. ET · TV: SEC Network

What's at stake for Kentucky: This is a huge narrative game for Will Stein's Cats. Lose here and you're 7-4 — but just two overtime 2-point conversions away from being 5-6 and fighting for bowl eligibility. Win and you head to Louisville with a chance at an extremely respectable finish in the CFP rankings and a label as a real contender for the 24-team Playoff next year.

What's at stake for Missouri: The Tigers are playing for an 8-4 finish and a better bowl assignment. Missouri doesn't want to enter rivalry week on a three-game losing streak, which would guarantee a losing record in the SEC this season and spark narratives about Missouri having a tougher time competing going forward after padding its record with weak nonconference foes and some easier SEC East games in the past. Missouri's finish also could dictate whether Eli Drinkwitz is a legitimate name in the coaching carousel.

Key injuries: - Missouri RB Ahmad Hardy is back for a fourth game. Eli Drinkwitz said Tuesday he is "still a little bit limited." - Kentucky is thin at cornerback: Kevis Thomas is out and Isaiah McMillian is questionable after both joined Friday's report. Grant Grayton remains doubtful, per A Sea of Blue. - Kentucky WR Nic Anderson is off the report, and Missouri CB Jahlil Florence is up to probable.

The question: Is Missouri about to lose both its star backs?

Hardy was shot in the upper left leg in May. Doctors put a stabilizing rod in his femur and left the bullet in his body, Drinkwitz told ESPN's Marty Smith.

He missed the first seven games and debuted Oct. 31 at Arkansas. He has 10 carries for 41 yards in three games, a year after he ran for 1,649 yards at 6.4 per carry.

"Nobody knows," Drinkwitz said in late October about when Hardy would play. Days later, Smith reported Missouri's expectations were "tempered."

KRCG's Bryan Hudnell filmed Hardy heading out for warmups before that Arkansas debut.

Meanwhile, Jamal Roberts has 1,133 rushing yards, second in the SEC.

On Friday, Locked On Mizzou host John Miller asked: "Is it actually more likely that maybe Jamal Roberts goes to the NFL next season than Ahmad Hardy?"

Nobody has reported a decision for either back.

Losing both in one offseason would sting. Missouri does have its quarterback: Austin Simmons re-signed for 2027.

And CoMo has become a running back destination.

Watchability: 🥓🥓🥓🥓🥓 (5 of 5 bacon strips)

THE PILE-ON

National media vs. LSU

Paul Finebaum, on Thursday's show, taped before LSU reversed course on RJ Luis Jr.:

ESPN's Pete Thamel, in the same segment:

USA TODAY's Matt Hayes reported that a source said the SEC office "went berserk" when Luis played.

My take

The national media is taking its cue from the stakeholders at other SEC programs.

Can you ever remember two state attorneys general taking huge public shots at a program in the same week, one of them calling for its expulsion?

Early in the fall, the SEC and its members were mad when Lane Kiffin wanted to roster players who'd been in NFL camps. They're even angrier now, according to CBS Sports' John Talty. He said on air Thursday that a source told him LSU is "going to wish they were expelled."

Some of the national media takes are tame for once in comparison.

🔒💰 Wake Up SEC Mortal Locks

  • Georgia -15 at South Carolina
  • LSU-Tennessee UNDER 46.5 · Top pick
  • Arkansas +20.5 at Texas

We locked in these numbers earlier in the week. Saturday morning consensus: Georgia -14.5, LSU-Tennessee total 45.5, Arkansas +20.5.

Why the UNDER:

Tennessee is playing without two starting offensive linemen, its best wide receiver, its starting tight end and its backup running back — in addition to a quarterback who may not be 100%.

In a vacuum, that's terrible against a defense that ranks No. 7 in adjusted EPA per snap and No. 10 in SP+.

But the Vols have carried the football 93 times in the last two games and figure to be ultra conservative in this one, especially if Tennessee can avoid falling behind.

I don't see either offense doing much. Is LSU going to open the playbook in Knoxville against a raucous crowd with Leavitt coming off a three-interception game?

I expect the Tigers to come out flat after all the drama on and off the field in the last two weeks.

The market agrees with me: I currently have a point of value on the total.
