The Lead

Friday can end the SEC title race.

Texas clinches a spot in Atlanta with a win at Texas A&M (7:30 p.m. ET, ABC) and an Ole Miss win over Mississippi State (noon ET, ABC). Do both and Alabama and Tennessee are out no matter what happens Saturday.

Both favorites look sturdy. Texas is an 11.5-point favorite and Ole Miss is favored by 18.5. That turns Saturday's Iron Bowl and Tennessee at Vanderbilt into fights over a Playoff seed, not a trophy.

Also today: Eight SEC men's teams play on Thanksgiving and Texas and Tennessee are both top-10 teams. The Watch Guide is next.

MY TAKES

🐘 Where does Alabama land in the Playoff without a bye?

My take

Bama's chances at a bye are gone. But if it wins the Iron Bowl and misses the SEC Championship Game, I don't see the Committee forcing the Tide to go on the road in the first round, either.

  • That would require a slip to No. 9 or lower.
  • However, every number matters. The No. 8 seed in theory has a much, much tougher game than the No. 5 seed.
  • Bama won't host the G6 representative unless it gets some help at this point.

One wild card: I do wonder what happens to Texas or Tennessee if one of those teams goes to Atlanta and gets blown out.

🐯 What did losing RJ Luis cost LSU?

My take

Did the Luis fight cost LSU anything on the floor, or only in the courtroom?

Of course it cost LSU something on the floor.

  • He averaged 18.2 points and 7.2 rebounds for Rick Pitino at St. John's in 2024-25.
  • He'd have a chance to play at an All-SEC level and would've raised LSU's ceiling to legitimate Sweet 16 contender.

Bottom line: As of now they look like an NCAA Tournament team and not much more.

🏀 Ole Miss or LSU: Which hoops team gets the better NCAA Tournament seed?

My take

The case for Ole Miss:

  • Ole Miss (No. 19 in KenPom, No. 17 in Bart Torvik) ranks ahead of LSU (No. 29 in KenPom, No. 24 in Bart Torvik) on the analytics sites.
  • I watched the Maui final against Arizona and was impressed at how the Rebels competed without 7-foot-1 Florida transfer Micah Handlogten (still clearing academic hurdles and expected to be eligible by the start of SEC play in January, or before).
  • Chris Beard nearly put together an all-time run in the SEC Tournament in March as a No. 15 seed and has advanced to the Sweet 16 or further three different times (including a national title game appearance), something Will Wade has only done once.

The case for LSU:

  • I think the LSU roster is more talented even without Luis.
  • The Tigers certainly look better on offense.

Bottom line: I'll say LSU gets a better NCAA Tournament seed at the end of the season.

🥚 Could Mississippi State fire Jeff Lebby after the Egg Bowl?

My take

Do we know for sure that Mississippi State won't pull the plug on Jeff Lebby if he ... lays an egg (see what I did there?) on Friday?

  • The case for patience: I think he deserves another season with a healthy Kamario Taylor and Fluff Bothwell.
  • The problem: But there's no excuse for the way that offense has performed without its star QB. We're talking borderline Top 10 offense (+0.275 in EPA per play, eighth out of 138 FBS teams) to one of the worst P4 offenses in the country (133rd out of 138 in EPA per play since early October).

Bottom line: So yes, at minimum, Lebby will enter 2027 on a very hot seat after losing by multiple scores Friday against Ole Miss.

Top 5 SEC skill players affected by injury

Florida shut down WR Vernell Brown III for the season on Tuesday, announcing surgery on the PCL in his right knee. His news got me thinking about the SEC skill players injuries hit hardest this season. Here's my Top 5.

1. RB Ahmad Hardy, Missouri - An innocent bystander, Hardy was shot at a concert in Mississippi in May. He missed Missouri's first seven games. - He rushed for 1,649 yards in 2025 and was a Doak Walker Award finalist. - Since his Oct. 31 return, he has carried just 13 times for 49 yards in four games. His future is uncertain.

2. RB Kewan Lacy, Ole Miss - Lacy rushed for 1,567 yards and 24 TDs in 2025 and earned All-American honors. But the Rebels ran him into the ground: 306 carries and 29 catches. - He missed the Florida game with a shoulder injury, then dislocated the same shoulder at Texas on Oct. 24. - He's out for the season after 388 rushing yards and 5 TDs in six games.

3. WR Vernell Brown III, Florida - Brown wore Percy Harvin's No. 1 and drew the comparison early: 17 catches for 271 yards and 2 TDs in four games (15.9 yards per catch). He also returned two punts for TDs and averaged 34.6 yards per punt return this season. - He hurt his right knee against Ole Miss on Sept. 26 and missed seven games. Florida announced season-ending PCL surgery Nov. 24.

4. RB Matt Fuller, South Carolina - Fuller teamed with QB LaNorris Sellers as a surprise two-headed rushing attack. He ran for 530 yards and 5 TDs (5.6 yards per carry) and caught 18 passes for 130 yards. - A foot injury ended his regular season in the eighth game, a win at Oklahoma. Coach Shane Beamer says he could return for a bowl game.

5. WR Trell Harris, Oklahoma - How different would Oklahoma's offense look if Harris had stayed healthy? He caught 59 passes for 847 yards and 5 TDs as an All-ACC pick at Virginia in 2025. - He took a big hit against Michigan on Sept. 12, underwent an emergency procedure and later suffered an infection. - He's missed nine straight games after catching six passes for 104 yards and 1 TD in two games for the Sooners. Even without him, Isaiah Sategna III counts 56 catches and tight end Rocky Beers 49. Put Harris alongside them and the Sooners' offense looks even more dynamic.

Also considered: RB Hollywood Smothers (Texas), WR Mike Matthews (Tennessee), WR/KR Terry Bussey (Texas A&M)

The Dec. 3 vote stands

A federal judge denied the SEC's request for a restraining order, so the Dec. 3 vote on LSU's membership stays on the calendar. The SEC says it will vote as scheduled "based upon LSU's representations that there will be no interference," and Louisiana Attorney General Liz Murrill says the state would sue if the league expels LSU. "We would have to," she said.
