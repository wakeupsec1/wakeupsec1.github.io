The Lead

Texas is going to Atlanta. The Longhorns still have a lot to prove once they get there.

No. 3 Texas (10-2, 7-2 SEC) beat Texas A&M 16-6 at Kyle Field and clinched the second spot in the last SEC Championship Game. The Longhorns face No. 1 Georgia (10-1, 8-1) on Dec. 5 at 4 p.m. ET on ABC.

That ended the title race for Tennessee and Alabama before either kicked off. Both now play for Playoff positioning and rivalry pride.

Texas won with a defense and a kicker.

Arch Manning threw for a season-low 143 yards (18-of-34) and the offense scored one touchdown. Gianni Spetic kicked three field goals.

"We were not perfect," coach Steve Sarkisian said. "It wasn't clean."

Georgia beat the Longhorns 22-19 in overtime in the 2024 title game.

LSU (6-5, 3-5 SEC) at Arkansas (4-7, 2-6 SEC)

Line: LSU -14.5 · Total: 49.5
Kickoff: 12:45 p.m. ET · TV: SEC Network

What's at stake for LSU: A chance to avoid ultimate embarrassment. As the university (presumably) tries to fend off an angry SEC to remain part of the conference, Lane Kiffin's Year 1 started with talk of national championship contention after blowing out Clemson.

Could it end in an upset loss to lowly Arkansas and a 6-6 final record, including a four-game losing streak? Kiffin better hope not.

He can sell being two or three plays from the Playoff at 7-5 with the last-second losses to Kentucky and Texas.

What's at stake for Arkansas: A freeroll, essentially. Arkansas won a pair of SEC games, which is more than it appeared the Razorbacks would do a few weeks into September.

If it even plays a competitive, four-quarter game against LSU, that will count as somewhat of a moral victory after 43-10 and 45-17 losses in its first two P4 games this season.

Key injuries: Per KickoffStatus, which logs the SEC's availability reports:

  • LSU has eight players out, including LB Whit Weeks and WR Jayce Brown. WR Winston Watkins Jr. moved from questionable to out Friday night.
  • Arkansas lists three players out, two questionable and one probable.

🏀 KENTUCKY

Rupp Arena meltdown

Basketball coaches almost never get fired in November. Kentucky's crowd is ready to riot anyway.

Kentucky (4-3) lost 73-71 in overtime to Wichita State at Rupp Arena on Friday night. It was Wichita State's first win over the Wildcats.

The loss alone would have hurt. The final 35 seconds made it a meme.

What happened. Wichita State led 73-71 after a Dillon Battie follow with about 35 seconds left in overtime. Here is the sequence per the Herald-Leader and On3's KSR:

  • Milan Momcilovic caught the inbounds pass out of bounds after a UK timeout. Turnover.
  • Kentucky chose not to foul down two. About 29 seconds ran off before Wichita State's Jahari Long threw the ball away with 5.4 seconds left.
  • After an exchange of timeouts, Kentucky called a play for Alex Wilkins to inbound to Mason Williams, who would run out of bounds and receive the pass. Teams can move along the baseline only after a made shot. Officials called a turnover.
  • Wichita State then threw it away again. Ousmane N'Diaye stole it and found Momcilovic open on the left wing.
  • Momcilovic missed the 3 at the buzzer.

Wichita State coach Paul Mills said: "I was like, 'that's not legal,' ha. We're fortunate to be able to get that basketball back."

On3 reported a chorus of boos.

Coach Mark Pope took the blame in response to a question about whether it was a problem that no one in the huddle, including assistants, caught the illegal play:

The numbers. The ending is the headline. UK lost the game earlier.

  • Kentucky led 29-15 in the first half and was ahead only 31-25 at halftime even though Wichita State shot 7-of-34.
  • The Shockers outrebounded the Wildcats 55-36 and grabbed 23 offensive boards. Will Berg had 16 points and 20 rebounds.
  • Kentucky played without starting center Malachi Moreno (concussion protocol).

What they said. Reactions from beat writers and national voices:

  • Jeff Goodman asked in a post: "Does Mark Pope even survive the season as UK coach?"
  • Kyle Tucker wrote: "Kentucky is going to lose by approximately a thousand at Virginia next week. ... I can't see a scenario in which Mark Pope pulls out of this nose dive."
  • KSR's Matt Jones said: "Mark Pope drew up a final play that was against the rules. There is simply no excuse for tonight." Jones also wrote that he hopes fans stay respectful because "Pope is a good person," but that "BBN has a right to be upset."
  • Seth Davis asked: "Obviously this is on Pope but how does not one assistant coach point this out?"
  • Kyle Tucker also warned before Pope spoke: "Mark Pope needs to read a carefully prepared statement in the postgame and not veer from it. Given the thunderous boos coming through on TV, if he comes out and says something like, 'The goal is butterfly, but we are caterpillar,' Lexington burns."

Analysis. Programs almost never fire basketball coaches in November. Nothing in the reporting suggests Pope is in danger this week.

But the schedule gives no room to breathe. Kentucky lost to Kansas (89-69) and at Indiana (90-76) before this.

Next comes Wednesday at Virginia at 7:15 p.m. ET on ESPN. The Cavaliers rank No. 8 in KenPom.

Louisville visits Rupp on Dec. 12.

A loss at Virginia would be the third consecutive and would turn a bad night into a referendum.

The crowd already booed. That should worry Pope more than one illegal inbounds play.

⚖️ LSU

The SEC had its talking points before it had its lawsuit

Emails show the SEC planned the message before it filed the suit. That makes Dec. 3 harder to sell as a pure rules vote.

Front Office Sports obtained emails through a public records request that show the league worked with crisis communications firm Legend Labs during the football eligibility fight this fall.

The timeline:

  • Aug. 31: Missouri president Mun Choi forwarded a Legend Labs strategy guide to commissioner Greg Sankey and leaders at every SEC school except LSU.
  • Sept. 1: Tennessee chancellor Donde Plowman followed up with an ESPN interview she and Georgia president Jere Morehead gave. She attached a draft of Choi's op-ed, which USA Today published the next day.
  • Sept. 3: The SEC filed its lawsuit against LSU.

The published op-ed differed from the draft.

The draft called the players a "handful of individuals who are trying to change the rules." The published version called them "young people who have worked incredibly hard to pursue opportunities in sports."

The guide also tracked individual X posts. One came from Locked On LSU's Matt Moscona.

SEC presidents will consider terminating LSU's membership Dec. 3 after LSU played basketball player RJ Luis Jr.

Analysis. The emails do not prove the SEC acted in bad faith. A conference can defend its rules in public.

But they show a league that built its public case before it filed its legal case.
