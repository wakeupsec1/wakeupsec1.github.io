The Lead

Tuesday's CFP rankings put nine SEC teams in the top 25 and kept Alabama at No. 3. National voices spent Wednesday calling the committee broken.

Two of the sport's sharpest numbers guys say it got the SEC right. "The SEC has been the best conference in college football this year, and it isn't close," Bud Elliott wrote.

THE BIG ONE

The critics vs. the numbers guys

Alabama is 8-2. It lost to Tennessee. It sits six spots ahead of the Vols. It was the popular target.

The critics

On3's Ari Wasserman went first: "Alabama being No. 3 may be one of the most inexplicably ranked teams in CFP rankings history. It literally makes zero sense." His column the next morning said the ranking "undermines the entire process."

Joel Klatt: "Is this real life? Could this be the worst CFP Committee we've ever had?"

Pat Forde wrote that the committee "fired Indiana on its day off" last week. "For Alabama this week? The rules (again) do not apply."

The numbers guys

Bud Elliott's newsletter: "The committee loves the SEC. This year, I get it."

  • The SEC is 10-2 against the other Power Four leagues. The Big 12 is 5-7, the ACC 6-9 and the Big Ten 4-7.
  • The teams the SEC beat in those games are a combined 53-47. No other league's victims have a winning record. Count Notre Dame and the Big Ten falls to 4-10.

On 6-4 South Carolina, Oklahoma, Florida and Ole Miss sitting in the top 25, Elliott wrote: "Personally, I love it."

His read: the committee is rewarding hard schedules. "It's easy to complain about the bottom of the rankings. It's much harder when you actually try to build a better one."

CFB Matrix's Dave Bartoo argues consistency, not conference.

"Committee is using exactly the same ranking rules as the last 12 years. After 10 games each its pretty much in same order as every year."

The mess is the explanation: "Its the chair that makes it sound like a mess. Different narratives same rules."

His 12-year model "barely missed it in order" this week. His reply to Wasserman: "It makes 100% sense."

He isn't a blanket defender. He called last week's Indiana drop "a move never done by a committee in 12+ years and only defendable with a word salad." Same culprit: chair Hunter Yurachek's narratives, not the rules.

The ripple: On3's Brett McMurphy reported that an athletic director who served on the committee asked a university employee for the employee's own Top 25 every Sunday before voting. On3 didn't name the AD or say when the AD served, so it's unclear whether the AD is on this year's committee.

Members are supposed to build their own rankings. A former committee member called it "a huge red flag." CFP executive director Rich Clark said no rule bans it, but "we want them to be independent thinkers."

What moves Saturday

In the Metrics Consensus that Elliott cites, No. 21 South Carolina ranks 30th and Georgia first. He puts that at "roughly an 18.5-point gap on a neutral field, before home field." A blowout likely knocks the Gamecocks out.

LSU is unranked but seventh in the Metrics Consensus. "If it wins in Knoxville, it should jump into the rankings, and nobody should be surprised," Elliott wrote.

HOOPS

No. 1 falls in Vegas. Luis plays in Baton Rouge.

No. 8 Houston beat No. 1 Florida 86-76 in a Players Era 8 semifinal. Dedan Thomas Jr. scored 30 with no turnovers and no fouls. No one else has done that against an AP No. 1 team in 20 seasons per ESPN Research.

Houston won the paint 44-18 against "the nation's best frontcourt." Florida made 15 3s, its most in 75 games, and still lost by 10.

My take

College basketball's preseason Big 3 -- Duke, Florida and Illinois -- lost a combined four games before Nov. 19. If that's an indication of the season ahead, it could be wilder than '26 college football.

I'm not concerned about Florida. Kelvin Sampson cobbles together perpetual Final Four contenders.

And the Cougars attempted 18 more free throws than the Gators (and made 25 of 27), went +6 on the glass and still led by just single digits with 1:14 left. Not many teams will outrebound Florida while tripling their free-throw attempts.

Later Wednesday, RJ Luis Jr. checked in for LSU with 12:04 left in the first half. He finished with 11 points and 9 rebounds in 15 minutes off the bench in a 79-67 win over Louisiana-Monroe.

My take

Basketball only, it was LSU's worst game of the season. The Tigers won by 12 against a team outside the KenPom Top 300 and fell nine spots. (Might the Tigers have been a bit distracted?)

But Luis Jr. managed 11 points and 9 rebounds in just 15 minutes. Double his minutes with the same production and he's playing at an All-SEC level, cold, in his first game.

How many teams are bringing the former Big East Player of the Year off the bench? At its best, this lineup can make noise in the SEC -- assuming Luis Jr. is clear of legal hurdles (seems like a big assumption).

IN COURT

LSU vs. the SEC, Day 2: the juicy details

In Birmingham: U.S. District Judge Annemarie Carney Axon rebuked LSU's lawyers for writing that SEC citations were "negligently supplied or hallucinated." The SEC had disclosed that it used the AI tool Harvey and certified that its lawyers checked the work. LSU attorney Bruce Rogers conceded: "They're not made-up cases."

Axon: "You put the pig out there. There's no time for you to put lipstick on it." She ordered "heartfelt" and "robust" statements of regret. She did not rule on LSU's motion to dismiss.

Murrill says no: Louisiana Attorney General Liz Murrill told the judge her office won't comply. "We cannot apologize for zealous representation." LSU did file to swap out the wording.

Georgia's AG weighs in: Christopher Carr wrote Greg Sankey urging sanctions on LSU "up to and including expulsion." "LSU is again attempting to benefit from the legal chaos it helped to coordinate and encourage," Carr wrote.

The contempt motion: Luis Jr.'s lawyers asked a Baton Rouge judge to hold the SEC and Sankey in contempt over Tuesday's statement. Attorney Tony Clayton told On3 he'd want "to talk to all the presidents and see if he called them." Sankey could end up back on the stand per Matt Moscona.

Meanwhile in Tucson: Arizona's RJ Godfrey won a restraining order and an injunction. The NCAA still didn't clear him to play.

WATCH THIS SPACE

Sellers is off the report

LaNorris Sellers isn't on South Carolina's availability report. ESPN says that signals he will play against No. 1 Georgia. DE Dylan Stewart and WRs Mazeo Bennett and Nyck Harbor are questionable.

Sellers was listed probable before the 34-6 loss at Arkansas and didn't play. The SEC defines probable as "likely to play, barring any setbacks."

My take

I'm still stuck on the confusion around the game in Fayetteville. First, Sellers was "probable to be available," which is not how the injury report is supposed to work, I don't think?

Then came the emergency talk, which apparently started with the SEC Network broadcast. Shane Beamer later said, "I never used the word 'emergency' with him."

At the time, everyone was thinking: what, trailing SEC bottom-feeder Arkansas on the road with three losses already and your College Football Playoff hopes on the line doesn't constitute an emergency?

Anyway, Beamer historically thrives at getting his Gamecocks emotionally up for big games and upset chances. But this may be his toughest motivational job yet.

The defense seemed totally deflated last week and seemed to let go of the rope once it became apparent the offense couldn't do a thing without its QB1 and RB1.

Now, with the meaning stripped out of the rest of the season, it's supposed to upset a team playing better than anyone in the country right now?

In Knoxville: Tennessee starting left guard Nic Moore is expected to miss the last two regular-season games per CBS Sports' Matt Zenitz.

The Vols have lost 10 players for the season. They include starting LG Wendell Moe Jr., starting TE Ethan Davis, WR Mike Matthews and backup RB Daune Morris.

Now they're down another starting lineman against a defense 10th in SP+.

LSU star LB Whit Weeks is expected to miss the game per Zenitz. "Looking increasingly possible he doesn't play again this season." The first SEC report lists him doubtful.

LANE BEING LANE

Kiffin's Wednesday

Lane Kiffin met reporters on a Wednesday teleconference before LSU's trip to Tennessee. He rarely passes up a headline.

  • On RJ Luis Jr.: "In no planet am I touching that." He passed along an LSU SID joke hinting at independence: "Maybe we'll get Notre Dame's schedule now."
  • On Neyland: LSU lost 32-24 at Ole Miss on Sept. 19 in his return to Oxford. Kiffin doesn't expect Knoxville to match it: "I'm not challenging Tennessee, I just don't think you can top that from just the feeling of what that was like, just the anger and hatred because it was so recent."
  • On Dave Aranda: Baylor fired Aranda in October. He has been around LSU this month in an advisory role per The Advocate. Nick Saban did the same for Kiffin after USC fired him in 2013. Kiffin: "I know what it's like to be fired in season and sit around in that town that you're fired in." Kiffin said he also brought in a fired SEC coordinator last week but wouldn't name him.

ROSTER WATCH

AK Dear is gone at Alabama

Running back AK Dear is no longer with the program per Kalen DeBoer. The sophomore played three snaps this season. He was the No. 2 RB in the 2025 class.

My take

We expected him to hit the transfer portal. But the timing is curious with two weeks left in the regular season.

Bama recently lost a 4-star RB commit to Ohio State and saw a backup target pick Florida. And the fan base is grumbling about Ryan Grubb's play-calling and the regression of the running game.

Still, the unit looks to be in great shape next season with promising true freshmen EJ Crowell and Trae'shawn Brown, plus the possible return of 234-pound bruiser Daniel Hill.
