The Lead

The CFP committee moved Texas and Tennessee ahead of Alabama. The Vols beat the Tide in Knoxville, but it took the committee four rankings to value head-to-head.

I'd call that overdue.

Texas is No. 3 and Tennessee is No. 5. No. 6 Alabama (9-2) fell three spots.

Also this morning: A federal judge hears the SEC's bid to clear the way for an LSU expulsion vote, and Michigan backed out of hiring Texas A&M's athletic director a week after both sides signed. Both stories are below.

CFP WEDNESDAY

Why Alabama fell

The latest: Alabama's three best SEC wins (Kentucky, South Carolina and LSU) are a combined 1-8 in November. All three have left the rankings, so the Crimson Tide have no wins over ranked teams.

Alabama counts three wins over Power 4 teams above .500, half of Georgia's total. Tennessee owns four, including its 16-point win over Alabama.

Texas (9-2) boasts a nation-leading seven wins over FBS teams with winning records. At No. 3, the Longhorns sit higher than any two-loss team has in a fourth ranking.

So now what? Alabama's no-win Iron Bowl

  • Alabama can't fix this Saturday. Auburn (7-4) isn't in the Top 25, so the Iron Bowl can only hurt the Tide.
  • If I'm Alabama, that's a scary spot: nothing to gain, a lot to lose. (Counterpoint: the Tide are 15-point home favorites at Bookmaker as of late Tuesday night.)
  • Texas is Georgia's most likely opponent in Atlanta. Pro Football Network's model gave the Longhorns a 79% chance to reach the SEC Championship Game entering the week. Tennessee sat at 13.8% and Alabama at 7.2%.

Bottom line: The Tide didn't lose last week. Its best wins did.

LSU

LSU asks the judge to end it before the SEC votes

Will the SEC really vote LSU out? Wednesday's hearing won't answer that.

It could decide where the fight goes after the vote.

The latest: LSU asked a federal judge Tuesday to throw out the SEC's lawsuit. U.S. District Judge Annemarie Carney Axon will hear the SEC's request for an injunction at 11 a.m. ET Wednesday in Birmingham.

What each side wants

  • The SEC wants the judge to overrule the state-court orders it says block a Dec. 3 vote by its presidents on expelling LSU, AL.com reports. Commissioner Greg Sankey said in a sworn affidavit that the vote stays on the schedule whether or not LSU keeps RJ Luis out of games.
  • LSU wants the lawsuit thrown out. Its lawyers say the SEC sued state officials, including Will Wade, who are protected by sovereign immunity. LSU says it has "taken no actions to prevent the SEC from holding any meeting it is entitled to under the SEC bylaws." It also says Luis is off the roster.

So now what? The predictions don't agree

  • Brian Kelly, on SiriusXM: "I will actually be surprised if they don't vote LSU out."
  • ESPN's Pete Thamel, on Finebaum: LSU "needs to genuflect & say they're sorry." Thamel also said it "would be shocking" if the SEC votes LSU out, "but it's not a nonzero chance."
  • USA Today's Blake Toppmeyer called an expulsion "stupid on steroids."
  • Locked On LSU's Matt Moscona says the SEC "will lose" in court if it votes LSU out. He adds that a school voted out gets no mandatory unwind period, unlike one that leaves on its own. Moscona says he is not a legal expert.
  • Attorney Tony Clayton, who represents Luis, told ESPN: "Mr. Sankey will feel the wrath of RJ Luis, if not by Christmas, then by New Year's night."

I lean toward Thamel's lane: unlikely, but not zero.

Bottom line: LSU says it already backed down. The SEC says that gives it no comfort. Dec. 3 is the day one of them is wrong.

TEXAS A&M

Michigan signed Texas A&M's AD. Then it backed out.

Texas A&M lost its athletic director to Michigan a week ago. Now Michigan doesn't want him.

The latest: Trev Alberts and Michigan signed an agreement on Nov. 17 making him the Wolverines' AD starting Dec. 1, the Detroit Free Press reports. On Tuesday, Michigan's president and president-elect wrote that his background check was "not acceptable to the University."

MLive reports Michigan decided that Alberts' deposition in a 2024 Title IX lawsuit at Nebraska disqualified him. The case involves a former assistant coach's sexual relationship with a player. Asked whether that kind of relationship is ever appropriate, Alberts answered in part, "it just depends," per the Free Press. He called the characterization of his words "wrong and unfair." Alberts and Nebraska deny mishandling the case in court filings, the Free Press reports.

His lawyer, Tom Mars, says Michigan owes Alberts more than $17 million, per ESPN's Pete Thamel. Michigan also agreed to pay Texas A&M a $4 million buyout.

So now what? A fight over one word: cause

  • Michigan: The letter says the background check "provides good cause to terminate the MOU." Fired for cause, Alberts gets nothing.
  • Alberts: The MOU calls itself "a binding contract" and never defines "cause." Fired without cause, he gets his remaining salary.
  • The lawyers: Employment lawyer Deborah Gordon called Michigan's letter "pathetic." Lawyer Sarah Prescott said the "good cause" argument is stronger and both sides have reason to settle.
  • Texas A&M: President Susan Ballabina named John Thornton interim AD, starting Dec. 1, and the school is picking a search firm.

Bottom line: Michigan says a background check ended the deal. Alberts says a signed deal is a signed deal. Either way, Texas A&M needs a new AD.

IRON BOWL WEEK

From scout team to Iron Bowl X-factor

If you're not an Auburn fan, chances are you've never heard of true freshman linebacker Shadarius Toodle.

Shadarius Toodle at a glance

  • Who: Auburn true freshman linebacker, No. 24, 6-foot-3, 235 pounds, from Mobile, Ala. (Cottage Hill Christian Academy)
  • Recruiting: consensus 4-star prospect, per Auburn's bio
  • Arkansas debut: 11 tackles, 4.5 TFLs, 3 sacks, 1 pass breakup. The three sacks are the most by a true freshman in a game in Auburn history.
  • Mississippi State: 10 tackles, 2 TFLs, 1 sack, 1 forced fumble
  • Samford: 3 tackles, 2 pass breakups
  • Season (3 games): 24 tackles, 6.5 TFLs, 4 sacks, 3 pass breakups, 1 forced fumble
  • Iron Bowl: Saturday, 7:30 p.m. ET on ABC, per AL.com. He is listed behind De'Marcus Riddick and Michael Matthews-Canty on the depth chart.

How he got here

Early this season, Toodle was a scout-team player with almost no path to significant playing time.

But preseason All-SEC linebacker Xavier Atkins got dismissed from the team mid-season (discipline). Then Bryce Deas suffered a season-ending injury and De'Marcus Riddick missed two games after a procedure on his ankle.

The breakout

Pressed into duty, Toodle exploded onto the scene with 24 tackles, 6.5 TFLs, 4 sacks, 3 pass breakups and 1 forced fumble. In three games! (For context, Bama's Yhonzae Pierre, a potential Day 2 NFL draft pick, claims 6.5 sacks and 8.5 TFLs in 11 games.)

Before the Arkansas game, Auburn defensive coordinator DJ Durkin almost started to worry about his quiet freshman. "I went to him several times [pregame], like, 'Hey man, making sure you're good,'" Durkin said. "And I'd just get a response back of, 'Yeah, I'm fine.'"

Boy, was he: 11 tackles, 4.5 TFLs and 3 sacks in his college football debut.

What's next

Now he could be an unsung hero of the Iron Bowl as the Tigers try to ruin the Tide's comfortable place in the College Football Playoff rankings. Auburn still lists Toodle behind Riddick and Michael Matthews-Canty on the depth chart. And Riddick "is fighting like crazy to make sure he can go," Alex Golesh said Monday.

But given how he played against Arkansas, Mississippi State and Samford, it's easy to imagine Toodle getting a role regardless.

LB Elijah Melendez (team-best 74 tackles, 10 TFLs, 4 sacks, 1 INT) and Deas (39 tackles) are just sophomores. So it's easy to see the trio becoming the best linebacker trio in the SEC next season.

Losing Atkins under semi-mysterious circumstances seemed disastrous for Auburn at the time, at least to this outsider. But it's turned into a blessing, allowing the team to incubate its talented underclassmen.
