The Lead

The SEC set a date to decide whether LSU stays in the league.

The presidents meet Dec. 3 to consider terminating LSU's membership. The league threatened it before.

Ross Dellenger reports that people in the league, "and even some at LSU," believe the presidents have the votes.

CBS Sports' John Talty and Matt Norlander add key context: expulsion is still considered unlikely, though frustration runs deep enough that at least some presidents could vote yes.

Why the SEC went to the nuclear option: league leaders approved penalties this summer for rostering former pros, including a half-season coach suspension and a fine of up to 50% of a sport's annual budget. A Louisiana judge's injunction blocked the SEC from penalizing or fining LSU over Luis, leaving the league with "no avenue to penalize" the school short of expulsion.

Why now

LSU coach Will Wade played RJ Luis last week.

SEC commissioner Greg Sankey said in a court declaration that AD Verge Ausberry and Wade had told him Luis would not play.

The SEC amended its complaint to add Wade as a defendant.

LSU president Wade Rousse since ordered Luis benched. That didn't end it.

Luis and Jack Pyburn haven't withdrawn their lawsuits against the SEC.

Late Monday, attorney Tony Clayton told ESPN that RJ Luis Jr. will sue the SEC and Sankey for damages — "a large number" — and will keep his contempt case against Sankey alive.

Clayton also said he plans to depose every SEC president and sent preservation letters looking for evidence Sankey organized a leaguewide boycott of LSU.

The off-ramp

Dellenger writes that the SEC has put LSU on the clock: "You have 10 days."

Ten days for what? That's how long LSU has, until the Dec. 3 vote, to get the Luis and Pyburn lawsuits dismissed. Dellenger asks whether the vote is the SEC's way to force that.

One person in Louisiana floated a way out. LSU would accept sanctions, such as a multi-game Wade suspension, probation or forfeits.

"It's unclear what SEC presidents will accept," Dellenger writes.

CBS Sports also reports one likely SEC demand for peace: concrete assurances that Louisiana governor Jeff Landry will no longer dictate how LSU follows SEC bylaws.

The two sides nearly struck a truce last month, Dellenger reports. They couldn't agree.

The reaction

Kyle Tucker: "In the staring contest with LSU, the SEC appears willing to cut off its own eyelids."

He also said the league might want to address "potentially catastrophic NIL contract language" first.

Matt Hayes: "Did the university really think this would end any other way?"

Brett McMurphy kept it short: "Buh-bye."

Brandon Marcello: "If LSU were expelled from SEC, I think they'd be fine seeking their own media deals as an independent, biding their time until the next iteration of college football in the 2030s as the P4 media deals expire. Scheduling in the interim, however, could be difficult."

THE SCHUMANN HIRE

Florida State gets its man. Georgia keeps him, for now.

Florida State hired Georgia defensive coordinator Glenn Schumann as its head coach on a five-year deal Monday.

ESPN's Pete Thamel reported that Schumann is expected to stay with Georgia through the postseason.

FSU athletic director Jeff Purinton has known him since 2008.

Schumann thanked "Coach Smart and Coach Saban for their trust in me."

Georgia coach Kirby Smart said earlier Monday that Schumann "has earned every bit of it," then added: "But the University of Georgia's focus is on Georgia Tech."

The 2015 blueprint

Smart did this before.

After Georgia hired him in December 2015, he kept coaching Alabama's defense through the Jan. 11 title game while recruiting for the Bulldogs.

"I want to do the right thing by both employers," Smart said then.

Chris Low says it's "much more challenging now with the transfer portal."

What Smart took with him

Staff came first. Schumann was one of two Alabama staffers Smart targeted. His salary jumped from $80,000 to $225,000.

The other, strength coach Scott Cochran, said no in 2015. Smart hired him away in 2020.

Alabama assistant head coach Mel Tucker became Georgia's defensive coordinator the day after the title game.

Then there's the recruiting board.

In January 2018, The Tuscaloosa News' Aaron Suttles reported that Smart took a picture of Alabama's recruiting board on his way out and showed it to recruits who weren't at the top of it.

Multiple people close to Alabama's program told that story. None would go on the record.

AL.com's John Talty reported that people inside the program believe Smart used Alabama's recruiting evaluations against Nick Saban, telling mutual recruits where they stood. Some there blamed it for a flip by receiver Tyler Simmons.

Matt Hayes later wrote it as fact: Smart "snapped a picture of the Alabama recruiting board" the day he left.

The Georgia coach who denied it to AL.com in 2018? Glenn Schumann.

"I don't recall that being done at all," he said.

My take

I was wrong to assume he wouldn't coach Georgia through the Playoff in yesterday's brief. But wow, that's going to be tough.

He has the respect of his new AD and his former boss in Kirby Smart. But the portal opens Jan. 4.

When Smart left Bama, he took as much with him as he could, including the recruiting board (a photo of it, according to multiple people close to Alabama's program), some staff members and other odds and ends.

Florida State's roster needs serious, serious work in the portal, which is in such a condensed window now that it's more than a full-time job.

Assuming Georgia advances deep in the Playoff, how is that going to work?

And if Smart keeps him around, does that preclude him from trying to steal a few key staff members -- or even players from the roster?

How awkward will it get if he's actively recruiting a few Bulldogs, even bench players, to Florida State during the Playoff run?

LSU'S DC QUESTION

The defense isn't the problem. So why is Blake Baker on TCU's list?

The Athletic's Bruce Feldman lists LSU DC Blake Baker among TCU's head coaching candidates.

Baker's defense ranks second in the SEC in yards per play allowed, third against the run and second in sacks.

Chris Low said on Finebaum: "I don't think his defensive coordinator will be there next year."

Low also said LSU coach Lane Kiffin "has thrown his defensive coordinator under the bus unfairly this year."

Meanwhile, former LSU DC Dave Aranda has hung around the program in an advisory role since Baylor fired him.

Asked if Aranda could replace Baker, Kiffin said: "That was not what this was about."

My take

Former Baylor head coach and former LSU DC Dave Aranda already is hanging around the building.

Is Kiffin pulling a page from the Nick Saban playbook? (Saban famously -- or infamously -- brought in Kiffin as an offensive "consultant" before eventually hiring him as OC.)

I don't think Kiffin should get rid of Blake Baker in a vacuum. He's a respected coordinator.

That side of the football hasn't been the problem for LSU this season. And with so many problems to fix, continuity there helps.

That said, we know things don't happen in a vacuum. If Baker and Kiffin don't get along well, that's unfortunate, but a move needs to be made.

I just wonder if Kiffin is cutting off his nose to spite his face here.

EGG BOWL

Golding takes the call sheet back

Ole Miss coach Pete Golding will call the defense again Friday against Mississippi State. He handed it to DC Bryan Brown for the Wofford game.

"This week, I'm going to call this one," Golding said. He also committed to hiring a defensive play-caller for 2027.

Ole Miss had the SEC's No. 1 scoring defense in 2024. This year it ranks 11th, and last against the run.

My take

Golding is taking back Egg Bowl play-calling but plans to hire a play-caller for 2027. Is Ole Miss' defense a coaching problem or a roster problem?

Both.

The defensive line rarely played to its abilities this season (the LSU game was a rare exception). And the secondary got roasted for much of the year.

The talent level wasn't nearly the same as the last two seasons under Kiffin.

But overall, it looked too disorganized and gave up too many explosive plays.
