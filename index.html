The Lead

Tennessee beat Alabama 26-10. The committee has still kept the Tide ahead in all three rankings.

Tuesday (7:30 p.m. ET, ESPN) is the fourth test.

The Vols beat LSU 27-13 on Saturday and the Tide beat Chattanooga 47-0. Texas (9-2) also sits in that mix after beating Arkansas 45-3.

Here is the argument for why head-to-head isn't the only criterion, then the Schumann fallout and what rivalry week puts on the line.

HEAD-TO-HEAD

Why the Tide is still in this argument

Tennessee fans have a case, and they are making it loudly.

Tyler Vesely pointed out that Alabama's strength of schedule dropped to 18th and its strength of record to 6th.

VolSoup lined up the numbers next to Tennessee's (20th and 7th) and added: "At some point the head to head result has to mean something!"

Mike Wilson put it to the committee: "Your move, College Football Playoff committee."

The committee has already shown its hand.

Athletic director Hunter Yurachek told CBS the chair mentioned the Tide's point spread in conference games as "close to 23 points a game."

The committee's own protocol lists head-to-head as one criterion among several.

My take

The College Football Playoff committee uses a proprietary strength-of-schedule metric through SportSource analytics. Although the committee describes how it works in broad terms, including a 2025 change that gave games against strong opponents more weight, the exact formula isn't public.

But per Sagarin and FPI's strength-of-schedule and strength-of-record metrics -- taking the average of each -- Alabama (14.7) now ranks behind Texas (4.3) and ahead of Tennessee (15.3). Bama's average scoring margin in SEC games is 14.6 points, ahead of Texas (11.1) and Tennessee (10.5).

The committee mentioned Bama's margin of victory when answering questions last Tuesday night.

According to the official College Football Playoff website, "the committee will select the teams using a process that distinguishes among otherwise comparable teams by considering: strength of schedule, head-to-head competition, comparative outcomes of common opponents (without incenting margin of victory) and other relevant factors such as unavailability of key players and coaches that may have affected a team's performance during the season or likely will affect its postseason performance."

Based on the numbers we have, assuming the committee's internal schedule metrics aren't way off, Texas has a clear case to jump Alabama this week. Tennessee and Bama essentially tie in our informal schedule strength metric.

The Vols own the head-to-head. But the Tide have been more dominant than the Vols in SEC play.

Alabama also is 4-0 against common opponents with Tennessee, including road wins at Kentucky and LSU (Tennessee beat both at home) and a win against South Carolina (Tennessee lost to the Gamecocks).

Head-to-head matters, but it's not the only criterion, as much as some media members and fans wish it was.

I say Texas flips Bama. But I'm not so sure that Tennessee does.

The margin that existed in the committee's mind between the two teams certainly is getting smaller, though.

THE SCHUMANN WATCH

Georgia's DC and Florida State

Florida State's search is focused on Georgia defensive coordinator Glenn Schumann, ESPN's Pete Thamel and Andrea Adelson reported Sunday.

They cited "mutual interest" and said talks are expected to pick up soon.

The ties run deep.

New FSU athletic director Jeff Purinton worked alongside Schumann at Alabama. Schumann's grandfather coached on FSU's inaugural 1947 team.

Then came the handshake line.

After South Carolina's loss, Shane Beamer told Schumann: "Hope that FSU thing works out, if that's what you want."

FOX Carolina News caught it on camera.

My take

It's about time we get some drama with Georgia.

The Bulldogs mainly have avoided the injuries that have hammered the SEC's three other likely Playoff teams. There aren't any noticeable holes in UGA's on-field play.

But this year's College Football Playoff National Championship gets played Jan. 25. The semifinals are Jan. 14-15.

Assuming Georgia makes it that far, I see almost no way that Schumann can continue coaching.

He'll leave on much better terms than Lane Kiffin if he takes the FSU job.

He's going to the ACC, not to an in-conference rival. And he's a coordinator, not a head coach.

Even LSU let Ole Miss "borrow" coordinator Charlie Weis Jr.

But the transfer portal opens Jan. 4. The new FSU coach will need to go all out to try to reconstruct the roster. I'm not sure that will be possible while trying to game plan for the likes of Miami, Texas or Notre Dame.

Ultimately, Kirby Smart has enough of a pulse on this defense that I think he can handle it without Schumann. But it's worth watching.

THE CAROUSEL

Coordinator dominoes

Missouri's Eli Drinkwitz was on CBS's Nov. 1 list of FSU candidates. He said Nov. 3 he is "not a candidate at Florida State."

Pete Thamel added a Michigan twist on ESPN's GameDay podcast: "Eli Drinkwitz, I think, fits Michigan's sensibilities" if the Wolverines move on from Kyle Whittingham.

The SEC's real churn will come on staffs.

Connor O'Gara expects zero SEC head-coach firings and a busy coordinator market at Arkansas, Oklahoma and Texas A&M.

  • Georgia's DC job could open if Schumann leaves.
  • LSU DC Blake Baker is on Pete Nakos' FSU hot board.
  • Texas A&M OC Holmon Wiggins is "officially on the chopping block" after the Aggies gained 189 yards in a 28-7 loss at Oklahoma. Nakos reports Mike Elko wants to keep Wiggins on staff as a receivers coach.
  • Alabama OC Ryan Grubb's contract runs out in February. After running back AK Dear left the team, he went on TikTok Live complaining about Grubb's play-calling, and several active players on the live agreed. Some Bama personalities in the know have speculated Grubb won't be back.

Iron Bowl: Auburn is chasing a number, not a miracle

Alabama has won six consecutive Iron Bowls and leads the series 52-37-1.

Auburn's last win in Tuscaloosa came in 2010.

Alex Golesh has made Auburn bowl eligible in Year 1 and says only one game matters now.

My take

Golesh's Year 1 includes a road win at No. 1 Georgia and a 7-4 record. Is it still a success if Auburn loses its seventh straight Iron Bowl?

Yes. Auburn's season will be a qualified success even if the Tigers lose in Tuscaloosa.

The Vegas win total was 6.5, which Auburn already surpassed thanks to the scalp over No. 1 Georgia in Athens.

Nevermind how statistically fluky it was; if I'm not mistaken, it remains the least likely win in all of college football this season based on Bill Connelly's postgame win expectancy measure.

All that said, Auburn probably will be a fortunate 7-5 that's more like a 6.5-win team (or akin to the betting-market preseason expectation).

And Golesh's decision to import so much of his USF roster and wave goodbye to a lot of Auburn's best players with eligibility remaining from last season while mostly going with value in the portal can't be repeated.

This roster just isn't good enough to compete week-in and week-out in the SEC. The roster-building strategy must change this offseason.

Palmetto Bowl: Beamer's job security

CBS's hot-seat read says a loss at Clemson puts Shane Beamer "directly back into the job-security crosshairs."

My take

Shane Beamer is as up and down as any SEC head coach -- season to season, game to game and day to day.

One day after giving a weird and semi-confrontational answer on why LaNorris Sellers, listed as a senior on the team site, didn't participate in Senior Night, he issued an apology.

He's clashed with EDGE rusher Dylan Stewart publicly and occasionally gotten into petty tiffs with media members.

He's gone from Playoff contender to hot seat to Playoff contender and back to mediocre multiple times.

Lose to this Clemson, which has scored more than 20 points just once in its last six ACC games (against a nosediving Florida State), he deserves to be right back on the hot seat -- or worse.

Texas at A&M: Friday, 7:30 p.m. ET on ABC

Texas can make it a fourth consecutive win over A&M, and every route to the final SEC Championship Game starts there, Chip Brown notes.

A&M (4-7) is on a four-game losing streak.

Texas fans are piling on. Doc_Texas wrote that the A&M job was "probably too big for Elko."
