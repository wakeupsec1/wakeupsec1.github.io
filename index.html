The Lead

Less than 24 hours after RJ Luis Jr. came off the bench, LSU System President Wade Rousse instructed Will Wade not to play him.

"In my role as President of the LSU System, after further review, I have instructed LSU Men's Basketball Coach Will Wade not to play RJ Luis."

Luis's night against Louisiana-Monroe, and for now his LSU career: 15 minutes, 11 points and 9 rebounds in a 79-67 win.

Matt Norlander and CBS Sports reported the reversal followed a "looming and increasingly real threat" of SEC expulsion and talks of boycotting LSU once league play starts in January.

A high-ranking SEC source told CBS the expulsion discussion never reached that stage.

Yahoo's Ross Dellenger reported that Rousse and Greg Sankey spoke Thursday, 30 to 45 minutes before a scheduled SEC presidents meeting.

Some presidents were "poised to seriously discuss the prospect of voting to expel the program from the SEC." Others wanted at least a basketball boycott.

On Wednesday, Georgia's attorney general sent Sankey a second letter in less than three months urging "severe action," "including expulsion," CBS reported.

Luis's attorney, Elliot Abrams, called an expulsion threat "reprehensible." CBS wrote that LSU "might ultimately have to pay Luis millions for just 15 minutes of playing time."

Late Thursday, attorney Tony Clayton told The Advocate that Luis is no longer trying to play for LSU, quoting him as saying "the juice is just not worth the squeeze" and that he does not want to hurt his teammates, coach or school.

Luis's side plans to drop its contempt motion against the SEC while reserving the right to seek damages. Clayton called Sankey "not an honorable man."

My take

I know we're supposed to hate LSU for what they pulled and applaud that sanity won out. And that's reasonable.

But from a basketball standpoint, it would've been really fascinating to watch the wildest all-mercenary team the SEC has ever seen including Luis Jr.

LSU is still talented and will take their turn alongside LSU football as a villain, although a lesser one without Luis Jr. going forward.

THE POWER STRUGGLE

Is Sankey losing his grip?

Even before Wade Rousse blinked on RJ Luis Jr., Blake Toppmeyer's USA TODAY column argued that Greg Sankey is "losing grip on the SEC."

Another front opened Thursday over the league's new uniform NIL and revenue-sharing contracts, which agents are calling "fundamentally unfair" and refusing to sign over steep buyouts and portal restrictions.

If you want all the contract details, Max Olson has the full breakdown at ESPN.

My take

I don't take issue with Sankey personally. But I know I'm in the minority among fans and the media.

I'm not sure when, exactly, public perception turned on him. I think it was sometime post-COVID.

But certainly, many SEC fan bases strongly dislike him now.

Perhaps it's no coincidence as college football, particularly the SEC, becomes much more like the NFL -- out with the wholesome traditions, in with decisions made to max out every last dollar of profit, fan desires be damned -- Sankey's likeability rankings are trending toward NFL commissioner Roger Goodell.

THE CAROUSEL

If Michigan opens

No SEC head-coaching job is open. CBS Sports says this cycle "should be quieter" than the one that moved LSU, Florida and Penn State a year ago.

The "bombshell scenario" is Michigan, where incoming athletic director Trev Alberts leaves Texas A&M on Dec. 1.

Sources told CBS that increases the odds Kyle Whittingham is one-and-done, likely by retirement or a negotiated exit.

CBS listed Alabama's Kalen DeBoer and Missouri's Eli Drinkwitz among the names if that job opens.

DeBoer signed a seven-year extension last April at $12.5 million a year through Jan. 31, 2033. If he leaves for another school, the buyout is $10 million through Jan. 31, 2027, then $8 million the next year.

Among SEC coordinators, Georgia DC Glenn Schumann is "heavily linked with Florida State." Alabama DC Kane Wommack, a former South Alabama head coach and an Arkansas finalist last cycle, is among the "hotshot" names.

Elsewhere, Brian Kelly has already interviewed at Maryland and is expected to get a Florida State interview. Hugh Freeze "was the immediate name that came up" at Arkansas State.

My take

DeBoer would not be interested in my opinion.

Assuming a good portal cycle, his 2027 Bama team should be his best yet. It's positioned to be in the preseason AP Top 5, which if I'm not mistaken, has produced 10 of the 12 national champions and counting in the College Football Playoff era.

He just signed a massive extension last offseason.

(Granted, the buyout if DeBoer decides to bail is relatively affordable. So never say never. But he's got a great working relationship with his athletic director. He's got a GM he likes and trusts. And he's finally starting to settle in, in Tuscaloosa.)

Would Drinkwitz be a candidate for the job?

Missouri is positioned to make a Playoff run in the 24-team field in 2027, especially if it retains QB Austin Simmons -- a potential first-round pick in 2028.

But he's come close to maxing out what he can do in CoMo, and never seems to match up well against truly elite competition there.

If Michigan decided it wanted him, which I question, I could see him entertaining it.

KNOXVILLE

58 minutes and 11 Vols down

LSU (6-4, 3-4) visits No. 9 Tennessee (8-2, 5-2) at 3:30 p.m. ET Saturday on ABC.

Books list LSU as a slight road favorite: consensus LSU -1.5, including DraftKings and FanDuel, total 45.5.

On his radio show, Lane Kiffin said LSU has not finished games: "We're a really good team for 58 minutes."

Whit Weeks is officially out. Aiden Anding, who tore his Achilles in the spring, is trending toward being available.

Tennessee has lost 11 players for the season, including six starters. Starting left guard Nic Moore is the latest, out for the year with an undisclosed medical condition.

The Vols average 44.5 rush attempts a game.

My take

My raw power rankings make the betting line Tennessee -2.5. I adjusted it to Vols -3.5.

Why? LSU just lost back-to-back gut punch games at home to ruin their season and their $50 million+ roster.

Kiffin has publicly conceded fair criticism of Sam Leavitt's fourth-quarter turnovers while already pitching donors on 2027 (despite the mini rush he'll get from being back in Knoxville).

Tennessee's injuries are one of the most underdiscussed portions of the entire SEC season. Pretty remarkable what the Vols have done in spite of that.

And the Vols want to run the football 50 times in what should be a grinder of a game.

Will LSU be emotionally prepared for four quarters of getting punched in the mouth on the road in its current emotional state?
